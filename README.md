# TITLE OF THE PRODUCT


# DESCRIPTION

The developed model is a three-dimensional CAD component created using CATIA Part Design. The model consists of a curved outer body, a cylindrical front section, and a vertically oriented supporting handle. The geometry has been developed using various Part Design features such as sketches, revolutions, planes, sweeps, trims, and surface-filling operations. The model demonstrates the use of parametric modelling techniques to create a smooth and ergonomically shaped product with controlled dimensions and geometric features.

# CHALLENGES FACED TO DEVELOP

During the development of the model, several challenges were encountered in creating the required complex geometry. Maintaining proper alignment between different sketches and reference planes was important to obtain accurate proportions. Creating smooth transitions between the curved body, cylindrical front section, and handle required careful selection of sketch profiles and modelling features. Additional difficulty was experienced in maintaining continuity between surfaces and avoiding unwanted gaps or irregularities. Managing the sequence of operations was also important because changes to an earlier feature could affect subsequent features. These challenges were addressed through the proper use of reference planes, constrained sketches, revolved features, sweep operations, trimming, and surface-filling techniques.


# SURFACE MODELS


The surface modelling stage was used to create the complex curved portions of the product that are difficult to produce using simple solid features alone. Different sketches and reference planes were used to define the required profiles, followed by operations such as Sweep, Trim, Fill, and Revolve to generate the desired surfaces. Surface modelling helped achieve smooth transitions between the different sections of the body and provided better control over the overall shape. The completed surfaces were then refined and integrated to obtain a continuous and visually smooth product geometry.

# ASSEMBLY MODEL

The assembly model combines the individual components developed during the CAD modelling process to represent the complete product. The components are positioned according to their functional locations and are aligned using appropriate assembly constraints. The assembly allows the overall fit, orientation, and interaction between the different parts to be examined before manufacturing. It also helps identify possible interference, alignment, and dimensional issues at an early stage, thereby reducing the possibility of errors during physical fabrication.

# PROTOTYPE MODEL (Photo)

The prototype model represents the physical realization of the developed CAD design. The photograph shows the final three-dimensional model developed in CATIA and provides a visual representation of the proposed product geometry. The prototype stage is useful for comparing the physical form with the digital CAD model and for evaluating the overall shape, dimensions, ergonomics, and manufacturability. The prototype can also be used for identifying design modifications that may be required before producing the final component.

# INFERENCE

The modelling process demonstrates the effective application of CATIA Part Design and surface modelling techniques for developing a complex three-dimensional product. The use of sketches, reference planes, revolutions, sweeps, trims, and fills enabled the creation of smooth and controlled geometry. The challenges encountered during modelling provided practical experience in handling complex profiles, surface continuity, feature dependencies, and geometric accuracy. The completed CAD and prototype models provide a foundation for further evaluation, including assembly validation, structural analysis, manufacturing assessment, and design optimization.
